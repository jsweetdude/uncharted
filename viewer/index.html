Map

Every dot is one item in the collection, such as a talk or a paper, placed so that items about similar things sit close together. Clusters of dots are neighborhoods. The outlined shapes are candidate connections: spots where the collection has little or nothing, between subjects it does cover.

Drag to move, scroll or pinch to zoom, and pick a shape to see what surrounds it. Distances on this flat map are only rough; every number in the details is measured on the full meaning of each text.

Legend

Shapes are candidate connections, filled squares are drafts that passed, hollow squares are drafts to try again. Colors only tell neighboring neighborhoods apart and repeat across the map; the names on the map and in the lists say which is which. In loneliness mode, darker and larger dots have fewer close neighbors.

Drag to pan, scroll or pinch to zoom. With the map focused, arrow keys pan, + and − zoom, 0 fits the map, and N and P step through candidates. Esc steps back.

Details

Shows whatever you picked last: an item, a candidate connection, a draft or a neighborhood. Similarity runs from −1 to 1, and higher means closer in meaning; the corpus median beside it is a yardstick. A loneliness percentile of 95 means emptier than 95% of the items. "Must not copy" lists the existing items closest to a candidate, numbered as on the map: a new idea there should not repeat them.

Pick a candidate connection to see what surrounds it, or select any point.

Find an item

Type part of a title to list matching items. Pick one to see its details and center the map on it. "Show 25 more" pages through long lists of matches.

    Candidate connections

    Each entry is a spot where the collection has little or nothing written, next to subjects it does cover. A connection mixes one item from each of two related neighborhoods and is kept only when that pair is emptier than chance; a pair sits between two related neighborhoods; a blend mixes chosen items. An empty spot is not a good idea by itself: read what surrounds it and search beyond this collection before writing anything. Entries marked "not on the map" have neighbors too scattered to place.

      Drafts

      Drafts are texts someone wrote for a candidate connection and then measured against the collection. Passed means the draft landed in empty space and stayed on topic. Retry lists what was too close to existing items or too far from the candidate.

        Neighborhoods

        Groups of items about similar things, found without being told the topics. The words are the ones most typical of each group's titles. Pick one to light it up on the map and see its most central items. Items in no neighborhood are drawn in gray.
